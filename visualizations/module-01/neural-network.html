01 · The neuron

One neuron is a weighted vote

Each input is a piece of evidence. Each weight says how much this neuron cares about it (negative = counts against). Add it all up, add a bias (how easily it fires), then pass the total through an activation, the bend that decides how loudly it speaks.

Why bother with a bend? Without an activation, stacking layers collapses into one big straight-line formula, no smarter than a single neuron. The bend is what lets depth add power. You’ll prove it yourself in the playground by switching to linear.

02 · Layers

Stack neurons and simple votes become patterns

Neurons in a layer all look at the same inputs but weigh them differently. The next layer then votes on the previous layer’s votes, so early layers pick out simple signals and later layers combine them into richer ones. Click any neuron to see exactly how it computed its value.

03 · Learning

Learning = measure the error, then roll downhill

Nobody hand-sets millions of weights. Training repeats one small loop, thousands of times.

1

Forward pass

Feed an example in. The network makes a guess.

2

Loss

Score the guess against the right answer: one number, “how wrong”.

3

Backprop

Work out how much each dial contributed to the error (its gradient).

4

Update

Nudge every dial a little in the direction that lowers the loss.

↻ repeat on the next batch of examples, until the loss stops improving

Gradient descent: one dial, one hill

Swipe the plot sideways →

Learning rate (step size)

Landscape

Each step: w ← w − learning rate × slope. Tiny steps crawl; huge steps overshoot and bounce out of the valley. Tip: click the curve to drop the ball anywhere.

A real network has millions of dials, so the “hill” has millions of dimensions. The rule is the same.

① Why the bend matters

Circle data with linear activation: every layer is a straight-line formula, so it can only cut with one line. Then switch to tanh.

② Too few neurons

The spiral with just 2 neurons in 1 layer. It can bend the boundary only a little, so accuracy stalls well short of perfect. Capacity limits what can be learned.

③ Add capacity

The same spiral with 8 neurons across 2 layers. It usually cracks it within a few hundred epochs, often after a long plateau. Runs at Fast speed.

④ Learning rate too high

Circle data with a big learning rate (0.3). The steps overshoot, so the curves wobble or stall instead of settling. Then try a tiny rate and watch it crawl.

06 · From neurons to LLMs

Same machinery, billions of dials: predict the next token

A large language model is a neural network too. Text goes in as numbers, flows through many stacked layers of weighted sums and bends (plus a mechanism called attention), and out comes a score for every possible next token. As the Module 01 recap puts it, the model picks a likely continuation, appends it, and repeats.

Training is the same loop you saw: guess the next token, score it, blame the dials, nudge. It is just run on a vast amount of text and code.

  • Fluent ≠ correct. Output is probable, not verified, which is why specs, tests and review sit at the core of this programme.
  • Behaviour lives in learned weights. You can’t read rules out of a network, so you check its work like a colleague’s first draft.
  • Tokens are the unit. Inputs, outputs, limits and cost are all measured in tokens.
Recap

The whole idea in four lines

Neuronweighted sum + bias, then a bend
Layermany neurons side by side, each weighing the inputs differently
Networklayers stacked so later ones combine earlier patterns
Trainingguess → score → blame → nudge, repeated until the loss stops falling
Weight
A dial that scales one input. What training adjusts.
Bias
An extra dial that shifts when a neuron fires.
Activation
The bend applied to the weighted sum. Without it, depth adds nothing.
Loss
One number for “how wrong is the network on this data”.
Gradient
The slope of the loss for one dial: which way to move it, and how steeply.
Learning rate
How big a nudge to take each step. Too small crawls; too big overshoots.
Backpropagation
Chain-rule bookkeeping that computes every gradient in one backward sweep.
Epoch
One full pass over the training data.
Overfitting
Memorising training data: train loss keeps falling while test loss stops improving or rises.
Parameters
All the weights and biases, the “size” people quote for a model.
Softmax
Turns raw scores into probabilities that add to 100%.
Token
The unit of text an LLM reads and writes, and the unit of limits and cost.
